### 🟢 1. The Setup

* The screen shows a **side view** of several **horizontal platforms** scrolling from right to left.
* You control a little character who **automatically runs** (or the screen scrolls, forcing you forward).
* There are **two buttons** only:

  1. **Color button** → cycles the *current platform color* in order: **blue → yellow → red → blue...**
  2. **Jump button** → makes your character jump.

At the start, you’re standing on a **blue** platform. You can’t jump yet because you’re not allowed to until it’s red.

---

### 🟡 2. The Core Rule

> You can **only jump when the platform beneath you is red**.

So before each gap, your brain has to figure out:

* How many color presses do I need before it’s red?
* Will I have time to jump right after?

Example:

* You’re on **blue** → press color button once → now **yellow** → press again → now **red** → now **jump!**

Miss the timing? You’ll either:

* Try to jump on yellow or blue (and die immediately), or
* Wait too long and fall into the gap.

---

### 🔴 3. The Rhythm

The loop becomes a **mental rhythm game**:

* Watch colors.
* Tap-tap-jump.
* Tap-jump.
* Sometimes no tap, just jump if you’re already red.

Every platform requires **a different pattern** depending on what color you land on.
So your brain is constantly juggling:

> "Okay, landed on yellow → one tap → jump.
> Next one’s blue → two taps → jump!"

---

### ⚪ 4. Why It’s So Addictive

Every death feels fair — it’s your fault for miscounting or panicking.
It’s not twitch reflexes, it’s **mental translation speed under pressure**.
The color logic is simple, but **your working memory collapses** when things speed up.

Leaderboards or a timer (like the “07.46” in the image) give you that *“one more run”* loop.
